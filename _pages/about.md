I'm Minjae Joh, a data scientist and linguist based in Chicago. I recently completed an S.B. in Data Science (with Honors) and an A.B. in Linguistics at the University of Chicago, and I'm continuing there in the Master of Arts Program in the Humanities starting Fall 2026.

My research interests center on the linguistics of East Asian languages and on topics at the intersection of language and computation. I'm especially drawn to L1 transfer — how a speaker's first language shapes the way they acquire and process a second — and to what these patterns reveal about how meaning is learned. In the Language Processing Lab at UChicago, I build and analyze psycholinguistic experiments, most recently on how the Question Under Discussion shapes scalar inference in naturalistic discourse (see [Publications](/publications/)). Across this work, I like using computational methods to study how people interpret and acquire language in context.

Alongside research, I've taught data science tooling and Korean language, worked as a research assistant at Boston Consulting Group, and served in the Republic of Korea Army. I speak Korean and English bilingually, am fluent in Japanese, and enjoy picking up new languages.

You can find more about my background on my [CV](/cv/), or reach me by [email](mailto:johminjae@gmail.com).
